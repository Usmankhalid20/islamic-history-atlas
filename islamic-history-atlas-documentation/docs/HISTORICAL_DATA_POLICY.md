# Historical Data and Citation Policy

## 1. Evidence before presentation

Do not add a historical territory, ruler, date, or event merely because it appears in an unsourced online summary. Every published claim must have a traceable source.

## 2. Source hierarchy

Prefer, where relevant:
1. Peer-reviewed scholarship and academic books.
2. University presses and reputable academic reference works.
3. Established scholarly encyclopedias and museum/university collections.
4. Carefully documented primary-source editions and translations.
5. Reputable public datasets with clear provenance and licensing.

General-purpose encyclopedias and community-edited sites can help discover leads, but should not be the sole support for disputed or consequential claims.

## 3. Record uncertainty honestly

Use explicit labels such as:
- Well-attested
- Approximate
- Disputed
- Inferred
- Unknown

The exact vocabulary should be standardized before implementation.

## 4. Territory interpretation

A historical map is an interpretation of evidence. It may be unclear whether a region was directly administered, tributary, claimed, occupied, or under temporary influence. Store the relationship type and explain it. Avoid drawing a sharp boundary when the evidence supports only a broad zone.

## 5. Attribution and licensing

For every external dataset, map layer, image, or geometry source, record:
- Source URL and provider
- License and permitted uses
- Attribution text required
- Date accessed/downloaded
- Modifications made
- Whether redistribution is permitted

A public GitHub repository or downloadable dataset is not automatically public domain or free for any use.

## 6. Review checklist

Before publishing a record:
- Is the entity correctly identified?
- Are dates represented with the right precision?
- Does the cited source support the specific claim?
- Is the territorial relationship described correctly?
- Is the geometry appropriately cautious?
- Are conflicts between sources recorded?
- Are attribution and license requirements satisfied?
